M.Sc. thesis · von Karman Institute for Fluid Dynamics & TUM Chair of Turbomachinery and Flight Propulsion · May–Dec 2025

Mean-line design and optimisation of compressors for combined cycle hypersonic propulsion systems

An Air Turbo Rocket's compressor has to run from a standstill on the runway up to the ramjet transition Mach number, far from any single design point. Cycle studies for sustainable hypersonic transport need its performance map across that whole range, and running 3D CFD at every operating point is too slow for that. The work was part of the EU H2020 MORE&LESS programme, building on VKI's earlier STRATOFLY research.

What I built

  • An object-oriented Python mean-line tool that resolves velocity triangles, real-gas thermodynamic states (CoolProp) and preliminary blade geometry for an axial stage, working with air or hydrogen.
  • A loss model combining profile (Lieblein, Koch & Smith), secondary (Howell), end-wall (Aungier), shock and tip-clearance (Yaras & Sjolander) correlations.
  • Differential Evolution optimisation of flow coefficient, work coefficient and reaction, with diffusion-factor and De Haller constraints.
  • An off-design solver with fixed geometry and reverse kinematics that sweeps 80–120 % speed to build the full map, plus a geometry correction step so the design and off-design modes agree.

Results

Design point
14,000 rpm · 8.5 kg/s · ISA inlet
Efficiency
ηis ≈ 0.915, total pressure ratio 1.255
Loading
Rotor diffusion factor 0.429, stator 0.442 (limit 0.6)
Geometry
56 rotor and 60 stator blades, rotor solidity 2.49
Off-design
Pressure ratio 1.17 at 90 % speed to 1.31 at 110 %, no choke
Self-check
Off-design solver reproduces the design point within 0.64 % (PR) and 0.22 % (η)

Next steps I set out: validate the losses against 3D CFD and extend the tool to multi-stage machines. Supervised by Prof. Volker Gümmer (TUM) and Dr. Bayındır H. Saraçoğlu (VKI).

Semester thesis · TUM Chair of Aerodynamics and Fluid Mechanics · 2024

Physics-informed neural networks for compressible two-phase flow

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) can solve flow equations without a mesh, but they struggle at discontinuities. Compressible two-phase flow is full of them: every material interface is a jump in density and composition.

What I built

  • A four-equation two-phase model with a mixture equation of state, each component following a NASG equation of state under pressure and temperature equilibrium.
  • A step-by-step progression: a PINN for the diffusion equation checked against its exact solution, then a finite-volume PINN for compressible flow, then the two-phase case with a mass-fraction equation and loss.
  • The FV-PINN solver itself: HLLE flux reconstruction with a flux limiter, two-stage Runge–Kutta time integration and automatic differentiation.

What I found

The plain PINN overshot at the phase interface. I traced this to the formulation rather than the training, and coupling the network with a finite-volume discretisation, which enforces local conservation, removed the overshoot. The loss kept falling but never reached zero, which I reported as the method's remaining limitation.
